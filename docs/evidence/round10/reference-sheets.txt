  - heading "Sheets" [level=1]
  - generic: A sheet helps people perform a scoped task that’s closely related to their current context.
  - paragraph:
    - img "A stylized representation of a sheet extending down from the top of a window. The image is tinted red to subtly reflect the red in the original six-color Apple logo."
  - paragraph: A sheet is useful for requesting specific information from people or presenting a simple task that they can complete before returning to the parent view. For example, a sheet might let people supply information needed to complete an action, such as attaching a file or choosing a location to save it.
  - heading "Anatomy in page link" [level=2]:
    - link "Anatomy in page link":

  - paragraph:
    - text: In macOS, tvOS, visionOS, and watchOS, a sheet is always
    - emphasis: modal
    - text: . A modal sheet presents a targeted experience that prevents people from interacting with the parent view until they dismiss the sheet (for more on modal presentation, see
    - link "Modality":
      - /url: /design/human-interface-guidelines/modality?changes=_1_1
    - text: ).
  - paragraph:
    - text: In iOS and iPadOS, a sheet can be either modal or
    - emphasis: nonmodal
    - text: . When a nonmodal sheet is onscreen, people use its functionality to affect the parent view without dismissing the sheet. For example, Notes on iPhone and iPad uses a nonmodal sheet to let people format various text selections as they edit a note.
  - paragraph:
    - figure "The Notes format sheet lets people apply formatting to selected text in the editing view.":

      - generic: Because the sheet is nonmodal, people can make additional text selections without dismissing the sheet.
  - paragraph: There are several common buttons that help people navigate through and dismiss sheets.
  - list:
    - paragraph:
      - text: The
      - strong: Cancel
      - text: (or Close) button dismisses a sheet without saving any changes. This type of button is common in most sheets.
    - paragraph:
      - text: The
      - strong: Done
      - text: button dismisses a sheet after completing a task or explicitly saving changes.
    - paragraph:
      - text: The
      - strong: Back
      - text: button lets people navigate to a previous step in a multi-step flow or to a parent view in a hierarchy. It isn’t intended to dismiss a sheet.

  - paragraph:
    - strong: Display only one sheet at a time from the main interface.
    - text: When people close a sheet, they expect to return to the parent view or window. If closing a sheet takes people back to another sheet, they can lose track of where they are in your app. If something people do within a sheet results in another sheet appearing, close the first sheet before displaying the new one. If necessary, you can display the first sheet again after people dismiss the second one.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Use a nonmodal view when you want to present supplementary items that affect the main task in the parent view.
    - text: To give people access to information and actions they need while continuing to interact with the main window, consider using a

  - paragraph:
    - strong: Provide an alternative to the Done button.
    - text: If you provide a Done button, always pair it with a Cancel button to give people a clear way to dismiss the sheet without confirming or saving their changes, or a Back button to move to a previous step in the sheet. Relying solely on the Done button implies that completing the task is the only way to exit the sheet, which can feel restrictive or misleading.

    - emphasis: large

    - emphasis: medium
    - text: is about half of the fully expanded height. Sheets can have one or more custom detent values.

    - text: For example, a share sheet displays the most relevant items within the medium detent, where they’re visible without resizing. To view more items, people can scroll or expand the sheet. In contrast, you might not want to support the medium detent if a sheet’s content is more useful when it displays at full height. For example, the compose sheets in Messages and Mail display only at full height to give people enough room to create content.

  - heading "macOS in page link" [level=3]:
    - link "macOS in page link":
      - /url: /design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets?changes=_1_1#macOS
  - paragraph: In macOS, a sheet is a cardlike view with rounded corners that floats on top of its parent window. The parent window is dimmed while the sheet is onscreen, signaling that people can’t interact with it until they dismiss the sheet. However, people expect to interact with other app windows before dismissing a sheet.
  - paragraph:
    - img "A screenshot of the Notes app, with the What's New in Notes sheet centered on top of a dimmed Notes document in the background."
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Present a sheet in a reasonable default size.
    - text: People don’t generally expect to resize sheets, so it’s important to use a size that’s appropriate for the content you display. In some cases, however, people appreciate a resizable sheet — such as when they need to expand the contents for a clearer view — so it’s a good idea to support resizing.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Let people interact with other app windows without first dismissing a sheet.
    - text: When a sheet opens, you bring its parent window to the front — if the parent window is a document window, you also bring forward its modeless document-related panels. When people want to interact with other windows in your app, make sure they can bring those windows forward even if they haven’t dismissed the sheet yet.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Use a panel instead of a sheet if people need to repeatedly provide input and observe results.
    - text: A find and replace panel, for example, might let people initiate replacements individually, so they can observe the result of each search for correctness. For guidance, see
    - link "Panels":
